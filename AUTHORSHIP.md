## Decisions

Human decisions that shaped the instrument (evidence: the development session
transcripts, kept locally):

- **The concept:** a mix of a granular generator and a looper, where existing
  sounds are layered, launched as loops and sent through effects.
- **Eight slots** as the core unit, each one a layer/loop with its own granular engine.
- **Effects scope:** a *highly configurable* delay and a deliberately *basic* reverb.
- **Advanced features beyond a toy:** quantised launch, a resample looper,
  modulation, scenes and a limiter.
- **Interaction model:** everything draggable, with sliders and knobs as the controls.
- **A drum machine** added later, with its samples generated from text prompts
  through ElevenLabs, and then a configurable **shuffle**.
- **Release scope:** which sample sets ship publicly (the synthesised sets and the
  generated drum kit) and which stay private (recorded voice takes).

## Exercise of judgment

The author defined the scope and reviewed the result as it was built, giving
feedback on how it looks and how it works. That feedback steered the iterations,
for example adding the drum machine once the layering worked, then adding shuffle
control to it.

## Goal setting & direction

**For musicians and experimenters who want to make quick ambient music from real
or generated samples.** Drop sounds in, layer them as granular clouds and loops,
put a beat under them, and shape the result with delay and reverb, in a browser,
with no install or build step.

Non-goals: a full DAW, a sequencer for arranging songs, or a hosted service.

## Art direction

The visual language follows the author's "Workroom" style: warm paper background
(`#f3f0e8`), near-black ink (`#090909`), a single signal red (`#c4161d`) for
active and armed states, Manrope with IBM Plex Mono for values, square corners,
and dense, instrument-like panels rather than app chrome.

## AI implementation

- **Code:** implemented by Claude Code (Anthropic, Claude Opus models) under the
  author's direction and iterative review.
- **Drum kit** (`sounds/drums/kit/`): generated by the ElevenLabs sound-effects
  API from the text prompts recorded in `sounds/drums/manifest.json`.
- **Sample sets** (`sounds/dawn` … `sounds/harbour`): synthesised by
  `tools/earcons/generate.py`, an FM electric-piano script that an AI coding
  agent wrote in an earlier, related project (UI sounds for a voice
  prototype). The author did not design these sounds in detail; they are
  included as starting material.

**Jurisdiction framing (Germany / EU).** Under EU law a work is protected when it
is the author's *own intellectual creation*; German law (§ 2 (2) UrhG) requires a
*persönliche geistige Schöpfung*, a personal intellectual creation by a human.
Purely machine-generated output does not qualify on its own. The protectable
human contribution here lies in the concept, the selection and arrangement of
features, the interaction and visual design, and the iterative direction
recorded above. Code produced by the AI tools under that direction is offered
under the same license regardless, and no claim is made that individual
generated lines are separately protected.

**AI-generated audio.** The drum kit is AI-generated audio and is labelled as such
in the README and NOTICE, in the spirit of the EU AI Act's transparency rules
(Art. 50). Users who publish music made with it may want to disclose this too.

**Provider terms (recorded for transparency):**

- ElevenLabs: generated sound effects may be used per the plan's terms of service
